Marquee map selection

One drag on the map to select any number of features, instead of a click per feature. The first way to select in bulk directly on Google Earth’s 3D map, built to fit with every existing way to select, on web and mobile.

Role
Interaction design and front-end engineering, sole engineer
Product
Google Earth, web
Timeline
2026, live
Links
Bulk styling docs ↗

01Context

Google Earth projects hold the places people care about: sites, parcels, buildings, routes. On the map, those features could only be picked one at a time. The alternative was ticking them off in a list in the project panel, away from the map where the work happens.

Styling dozens of features meant dozens of clicks, and anything more complex hit a wall. Earth also needed a temporary drawing state for features on the way, and marquee selection would be the first thing built on it, so it had to set the pattern.

02Approach

Selection already worked three ways, and marquee selection had to become the fourth without breaking any of them. Select on the map and the panel updates. Change the panel and the map follows.

The hard part was everything around the gesture:

  • Web and mobile. A mouse drag and a touch drag mean different things, and on mobile a drag usually moves the map.
  • Streamlined and productivity layouts. Simple by default for new users, fast for people managing hundreds of features.
  • Screen-centered drawing tools. Measuring and drawing also claim the middle of the map, so the gestures can’t collide.

Every conflict was worked out by prototyping, testing and iterating with a UX designer and a PM. The implementation, end to end, was one engineer.

03Outcome

Marquee selection is live in Google Earth on the web. Select a whole neighborhood of features in one motion, then style them together. As the first feature on Earth’s new drawing state, it laid the groundwork for the drawing tools to come.
