01Google Earth · Interaction design, front end

Drag to select

Draw a box on the map to select as many places as you want at once. Before this, Google Earth only let you pick them one at a time. I designed it and wrote all of the code.

Role
Design and front-end engineering
Team
Me, with a PM and a UX designer
Platform
Google Earth, web
Docs
Bulk styling ↗

01Before

You could click one place on the map, or tick several in the project panel. Nothing in between. For anyone with more than a handful of places, it was slow, unintuitive, and a dead end for more complex work.

02Why now

Earth needed a temporary drawing state for features on the way. Drag to select was the first thing to use it, so it had to set the pattern for what came after.

03The hard part

Selection already worked three ways: checkboxes, dragging in the panel, and clicking items. The new gesture had to get along with all of them.

  • Web and mobile. One idea, two very different inputs.
  • Streamlined and productivity layouts. Simple by default, fast for power users.
  • Screen-centered drawing tools. Other tools also claim the middle of the map, so the gestures can’t collide.

There were a lot of ways to get it wrong. I worked through them by building, testing and going back and forth with the designer and PM.

04Result

You can now drag over as many places as you want and style them all at once, without leaving the map.
