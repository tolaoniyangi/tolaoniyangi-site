06engineering research · Sidewalk Labs · 2019

Estimating pedestrian activity

How busy will the streets around a site be? A pipeline that answers it from census data, as a “buzz metric” generative design tools can use.

Role
Computational design summer associate
Product
Generative design tools
Timeline
Summer 2019
Team
Generative Design, Sidewalk Labs

01Context

Generative design tools score design options against objectives. One question matters for almost any site: how busy will the streets around it be? Answering it needs data about who actually lives and works there.

Getting geospatial data into those tools was slow and manual, which kept them stuck on narrow test cases. The first step was research into which use cases mattered most, and which standard datasets, like pedestrian activity and transit access, they needed.

The research traced each kind of urban data, from weather to traffic, to the planning questions it could help answer. Building, parcel, boundary and census data fed the most questions, and all four describe who lives and works where. That pointed to the dataset built next: an estimate of the people around a site, and the activity they bring to its streets.

02Approach

An automated pipeline pulls the data in. Census population and workforce counts come in large areas called block groups, so the pipeline splits them down to individual buildings then estimates pedestrian activity on the streets around them.

03Outcome

The result was a repeatable, automated pipeline, built and tested on a site in Manhattan. It produces building-level population, workforce and land use, plus a “buzz metric” that shows pedestrian activity from residents and workers. It was built to plug into the generative design workflow, so the tools would not be limited to narrow test cases.
